# Lax collagen matrix and proprioceptive signaling

**Posted:** 2026-11-20  
**Status:** Designing  
**Tags:** proprioception · collagen · hypermobility · biomechanics · behavioral

## Open question

Does a lax or disorganized collagen matrix (and possibly residual strain-generated potentials, not bulk dry-tissue piezoelectricity) change how mechanical force reaches proprioceptors—helping explain poorer joint position sense in hypermobility?

## Why it matters

Collagen is piezoelectric mainly when dry; in hydrated tissue the effect is largely screened and streaming potentials dominate. PIEZO2-deficient humans (Chesler et al., NEJM 2016) retain proprioception, so collagen piezoelectricity is unlikely to be the primary signal. The open gap is mechanical: hypermobility is associated with worse lower-limb joint position sense in a 2013 meta-analysis, while Rombaut et al. found intact vibration sense but impaired knee repositioning, and Clayton et al. reported proprioceptive precision deficits that track hypermobility severity. A lax matrix might alter stress transmission to receptors even when large-fiber sensation is intact.
